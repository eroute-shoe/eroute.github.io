The Challenge

The Problem with Traditional Navigation

The traditional white cane is a trusted and important assistive tool. However, it has natural limitations that can restrict a user's confidence and awareness of their surroundings.

Provides limited information about the surrounding environment

Requires one hand to be occupied at all times

Cannot intelligently detect or warn about stairs ahead

Cannot send emergency alerts or share GPS location with family

Provides no lighting assistance in low-visibility environments

e-Route is not a replacement — it is a smart assistive layer that works alongside traditional aids to give users more information, more safety, and more independence.

AI Feature

AI Stair Recognition

e-Route uses an onboard AI camera module to identify stairs in the user's path before they arrive at the edge.

Detects ascending and descending stairs in real time

Sends a distinct vibration pattern to warn the user

AI scanning identifies stair edges before the user reaches them

Gives the user time to slow down and navigate safely

Ultrasonic Sensing

Smart Obstacle Awareness

Ultrasonic sensing continuously scans the walking path ahead. When an obstacle enters the detection range, the shoe warns the user through immediate vibration feedback.

Continuous forward scanning while walking

Stronger vibration as the obstacle gets closer

Detects concrete pillars, walls, vehicles, and other objects

Works hands-free — no action required from the user

Auto Lighting

Automatic LED Lighting

When the surrounding environment becomes dark, e-Route automatically turns on built-in LED lighting to improve visibility and safety — no manual switching needed.

LDR sensor monitors ambient light levels constantly

Automatically activates when environment turns dark

Makes the user visible to others in low-light environments

Integrated LED strip across the shoe sole for maximum coverage

Built and Tested

From Working Prototype to Final Product

e-Route began as a real, functional prototype using physical sensors, an AI camera, GPS, vibration motor, LDR sensor, LEDs, and crash detection hardware.

The prototype proved every core concept works. The final product concept refines these components into a protected, wearable, and professional shoe design ready for real-world use.
